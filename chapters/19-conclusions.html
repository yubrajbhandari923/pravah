21  Conclusions: answers to the central questions

Simulated passive tracer only. Nothing in this chapter is evidence about ambient PM2.5, real sources, exposure or deployment, and no result has been validated against observations (E18 was not conducted).

This chapter answers the five central questions of plan 3 (E19). Each answer gives effect sizes with their intervals, the sample unit, the experiment and the audit, and then alternative explanations and limits. Every claim has a row in reports/evidence-matrix.csv (claim ids in brackets), which links it to its table or figure, run record, commit and scheduler job. scripts/validate_report.py checks that those links resolve. Where an audit corrected a claim, the audit’s wording is used.

ImportantWhat scale each answer rests on
  • 64² (split VanLeer, protocol calendar): E07 sweep (40 scenarios, 10 seeds per cell), E08 sweep and its stage-2 variants (60 scenarios), E11 sweep (20 clusters), E17 regional case (3 cases × 20 clusters), and gate-09’s re-scoring of E07’s cached fields.
  • 32² pilots only (upwind numerics, 6 independent clusters, or n = 10 for E16): E09, E10, E12, E13, E14, E15 and E16. Their 64² runs are documented but were deferred, so these answers are pilot-level.
  • Smoke scale, exploratory: E02–E06 (the 1 g/s source scale was chosen after seeing data).
  • Confirmatory: one test only, E07’s dopt vs random, and only in the 8 cells that were run.

21.1 1. Sensor count, observation history and identifiability

Answer. How many sensors are needed depends first on whether they are placed with knowledge of the source locations. How much history helps depends on whether the wind changes and how long the sources stay constant.

  • Without placement knowledge, few fixed sensors cannot separate the sources. With ≤ 8 fixed, non-optimised sensors and a 24 h window, the sensitivity matrix was rank deficient in all 648 fits, at both source scales (E03, 32²; gate-03: baseline not cleared) [E03-1]. This is a negative result. The “identifiable recovery” half of E03’s hypothesis was never tested.
  • With location-aware placement, about one sensor per source is enough under a 24 h window. At 64², the median breakpoint k*(0.2) for near-source and D-optimal designs was 2 / 4 / 8 for K = 2 / 4 / 8 sources. Random networks needed 8 / 16 / 16, and coverage designs 8 / 8 / 8 (E08, 20 seeds per K) [E08-1]. The preregistered H1 (k* grows with K for D-optimal) was supported, with 19 wins, 0 losses and 1 tie [E08-2].
  • Elapsed time alone adds nothing; a change of wind does. Under an exactly constant wind, practical rank was the same at 6, 24 and 168 h in every seed. Under veering wind, the window gain beyond the constant-wind gain was +0.37 (bootstrap CI 0.24–0.49, 16/17 non-tied seeds, p = 3e-4; drifting wind +0.11, p = 0.02) (E05 v2, 20 seeds, 32²; gate-05) [E05-1, E05-2]. The v1 claims that a longer window improves rank steadily and can replace sensors were withdrawn: the effect came from a drifting control wind [E05-W1].
  • More sensors beat more readings, until the sources are covered. For random networks, 4k sensors × 6 readings beat k sensors × 24 readings (H4, 58/60). For location-aware designs this flips at large k: source_near 0/60 and dopt 5/60 from k = 8 to 32 (E08, 64²) [E08-5].
  • With all past data, sufficiency becomes trivial in this benchmark. Using an estimator over all past data, every strategy reached τ = 0.2 with k = 2 for every K (E08 stage 2, 64²; gate-12-17: a genuine floor, causality verified) [E08-8].

Alternatives and limits. - The all-past-data floor depends on strengths that are exactly constant for 60 days and on a perfect transport model. Time-varying sources (E10), wrong winds (E12) and model mismatch (E13) each remove most of the memory gain. - Sensors within 1200 m of a source sit inside its emission disc. Excluding those sites roughly doubles k* (source_near 4 / 8 / 8; random 16 / 24 / > 32) [E08-10]. - The E05 identifiability results are 32², use random nested sites, and use a source scale chosen after seeing data. - k* is defined with the held-out 1 h forecast and τ = 0.2. Other thresholds were not used as headlines.

21.2 2. Which placement is best for which task

Answer. Knowing the source locations is what matters most. Among location-aware designs, which one is “best” depends on the estimator’s memory. It does not depend on the endpoint, which in this setup barely discriminates.

  • Confirmatory (cells run). In the 64² sweep, the D-optimal network cut held-out 1 h forecast RMSE against the mean of 10 random networks by a median 42–84 % per cell. The 95 % percentile CI was above 10 % in 8/8 cells, the distribution-free CI in 7/8 (veering, K = 2, k = 16 straddles), and the mean bootstrap in 8/8 (10 seeds per cell; K ∈ {2, 4}, k ∈ {8, 16}; gate-07: pass with conditions; scope approved by the user) [E07-1]. K = 8, k = 32 and seeds 10–19 were not run, so at protocol level those cells are unresolved [E07-2]. The gain over random is attributable to knowing source locations, not to D-optimisation: a locations-only control did better still under the estimator of the test.
  • Under a 24 h window, near-source beats D-optimal. source_near won 115/120 paired comparisons (110 with AR(1) noise, 118 with per-site σ), with a median margin of 0.41 (layout bootstrap CI 0.28–0.56) and 20/20 layouts favouring it [E07-3]. E08 replicated this on 40 fresh scenarios (H5, 39/40) [E08-6]. Mechanism (exploratory): D-optimal networks lose practical rank in held-out southerly windows, a direction absent from training. At k = 8 their deficient-window fraction there is 0.37, against 0.01 for source_near, and 79 % of dopt’s pooled squared error falls in such windows [E07-4].
  • With all past data, the ordering reverses. D-optimal beat source_near in 103/120 pairs (median −0.25, CI −0.38 to −0.18; 19/20 layouts), and dopt beat random under both estimators (117/120 and 120/120) (gate-09, 64² cached fields) [E07-8]. E08 replicated the reversal on fresh scenarios (dopt 36/40, k = 2–16) [E08-9]. All 8 E16 ablation cells show the same flip, but those are pilot-level [E16-2].
  • Forecast-optimised placement wins only under the memory it was built for. Under a 24 h window, the wind-robust forecast design beat dopt even with an out-of-sample prior: +0.34 (CI 0.15–0.40, 19/20 clusters, Holm p = 0.002) at k = 8. It lost to source_near at k = 8 (−0.22, CI −0.54 to −0.08, 2/20, Holm p = 0.018). Under all past data it was worse than dopt (−0.30, CI −0.40 to −0.12, 2/20) (E11, 64², exploratory Holm family of 72 contrasts; gate-12-17: pass) [E11-2, E11-3, E11-4].
  • The endpoints agree, but by construction. The network best on forecast was also best on source error in 116/120 cases. All endpoints are functions of one static estimate under a perfect model, so this is not evidence against “no single network wins” [E07-6].

Alternatives and limits. - Source-disc artefact. With every site within 1200 m of a source excluded, near-source still beat D-optimal (0.375, CI 0.31–0.45, 104/120). But D-optimal’s gain over random fell from 0.71 to 0.20 (CI 0.15–0.33), and no cell cleared the 10 % confirmatory threshold. The confirmatory verdict holds only for the protocol’s candidate set [E07-5]. - The mechanism rests on one unseen wind direction; 12/40 scenarios show no southerly loss. - Every design is told the true source locations. No result here says how to place sensors when source locations are unknown. - dopt and the forecast designs are local optima of their criteria (greedy + swap).

21.3 3. Value of sequential updating and memory

Answer. Memory, not filtering, carries the gain. With constant sources, using all past data cuts forecast error sharply. A filter that only corrects the field does almost nothing. Letting strengths drift helps only for short horizons, and only when the sources really change.

  • Memory. The augmented Kalman filter equals the batch MAP over all past data (to 4e-13). It cut 1 h forecast RMSE against the 24 h window by a median 58–74 % (CIs 0.50–0.85; 10–12 of 12 scenarios per design; 6 independent clusters; 32²). Gate-09’s 2 × 2 control attributes a median 0.99 of the log-RMSE gain to the window, not the prior or the sequential form (E09; gate-09: pass with conditions) [E09-1]. On E07’s 64² fields the gain was 0.85–0.94 [E09-2].
  • Field-only filtering. A filter that corrects only the field was 1.6–13× worse than the 24 h MAP. Corrections leave the domain with the wind within hours [E09-3].
  • Drifting sources (pilot). Under time-varying truth, the validation-selected random-walk filter cut 1 h RMSE at k = 8 by 0.24–0.55 against all past data and by 0.30–0.42 against a validation-tuned window (6/6 clusters) [E10-2]. Under a diurnal cycle it was 12–15 % worse at 6 h and never improved source RMSE (gate-10/11) [E10-3]. With constant sources, q = 0 was best [E10-1].
  • Calibration. Under white noise the all-past-data filter is calibrated (95 % coverage 0.97 at 32², 0.95 at 64²). Under AR(1) noise it is overconfident (0.48), and NIS does not show it while the lag-1 autocorrelation does [E09-4]. The drifting filter’s 0.91–0.98 coverage holds only for an oracle-matched q. The deployable filter’s coverage was never computed [E10-4].

Alternatives and limits. - The memory gain is as large as it is because strengths are exactly constant for 60 days and the model is perfect. Under any wind error tested it mostly vanished: 0.60–0.77 with the true wind fell to ≤ 0.06 (E12, pilot) [E12-3]. In the regional case, a 40 % vector wind error removed it entirely [E17-4]. - E10’s time-varying truths come from the filter’s own model, which favours the filter. - Everything in this answer except E09-2 and E07-8 is 32² upwind with 6 clusters.

21.4 4. Reliability under wind and structural mismatch

Answer. Model errors dominate placement differences, and truth-free checks catch only some of them. These are pilot results (32², 6 clusters). “Persistence” below is oracle full-field persistence, the true field at t carried to t + 1 everywhere, a bound no sensor network can reach. “Worse than persistence” is therefore not “worse than a naive forecast”.

  • Wind errors. A direction error of 30° or more, a lag of 2 h or more, or AR(1) direction noise with sd 30° put the 1 h forecast below the persistence bound in 0/6 clusters. Speed underestimation kept skill (6/6) (E12; gate-12-17: pass with conditions) [E12-1]. For speed, +10° and AR 15° errors the sensor fit ratio stayed 1.01–1.33 while source RMSE rose up to 16× (compensation hidden in a good fit). Larger direction and timing errors degraded the fit 1.6–4.3×, which is visible [E12-2]. Under the wrong wind, dopt and source_near were within a few per cent of each other [E12-4].
  • Background and sensor errors. A known-background offset was fully repairable by estimating the background (skill −3.5 → +0.94, coverage 0.96, 6/6) [E13-1]. A common-mode sensor bias produces readings identical to a background offset (to 3.6e-15). The background term absorbs it into the forecast field while every truth-free check stays nominal: a structural silent failure (skill −3.9) [E13-2].
  • Source-basis errors (shifted, wider or missing sources) were not repaired by any estimator. Shifts left strong residual warnings (fit 1.3–3.5); wide footprints and a missing source left weak ones (1.12–1.27) [E13-3].
  • Self-diagnosis. The best truth-free early warning of forecast failure had within-configuration AUC 0.71 (CI 0.64–0.77; pooled 0.74), and 0.81–0.86 for large direction or timing errors (E14) [E14-1]. A × 0.8 speed error was undetectable (AUC 0.44–0.57) [E14-2]. An omitted source was flagged (AUC 0.84–0.89) by networks that sample its plume, but those networks were designed knowing the source. Random networks missed it [E14-3]. With a 30° direction error, 90 % coverage fell to 0.07 [E14-5].
  • Regional winds (64²). With the ERA5 point nearest the site instead of the box mean, or a 30° veer, every forecast was 1.8–3.7× the persistence bound. Forecasts using the true strengths were equally bad (0.047 vs 0.0465–0.0469), so this is a transport floor that neither placement nor memory can remove (E17; gate-12-17) [E17-4].

Alternatives and limits. The stress magnitudes are synthetic choices. The persistence baseline and E14’s within-configuration AUC were added after seeing results. The E13 field-level silent-failure rule is post hoc and has an open bug: 46 of its 65 flags are low-skill configurations under the oracle wind too [E13-5]. E14’s omitted-source detectability is biased upward by the design bug noted above. E12–E14 have no 64² runs.

21.5 5. Robust placement and transfer (E15–E17)

Answer. Scenario-robust placement bought no tail protection beyond chance. The main qualitative conclusions survived one-at-a-time physics changes at pilot scale. Under real-place winds, placement still beat random, but the D-optimal vs near-source distinction mostly disappeared.

  • Robust placement (E15, 32² pilot) — negative. The largest tail gain, CVaR-robust vs dopt at k = 2 under a 24 h window (+0.09, CI 0.03–0.12, 6/6 clusters, p = 0.031 uncorrected), cannot be told apart from multiplicity: 9 of 648 tail contrasts came out 6/0, against about 10 expected by chance (gate-12-17) [E15-1]. Robust designs behaved like source_near under a 24 h window [E15-2]. Built for the 24 h window, they lost badly under all past data (k = 2: CVaR −0.46, 0/6) [E15-3]. Mean, CVaR and worst-case criteria were indistinguishable [E15-4].
  • Generalization (E16, 32² pilot, n = 10 per cell, 35 uncorrected verdicts). No ablation reversed any of the five tested conclusions in 8 cells, two of which used VanLeer. D-optimal over random (nominal +0.79, CI 0.65–0.90) and the memory gain held 10/10 in every cell [E16-1]. The memory flip appeared in every cell [E16-2]. Wider sources weakened C1 (8/10). The “×20 diffusion” cell is only ×1.5 effective at 32² upwind, a weak test [E16-3]. The two VanLeer cells finished after the gate-12-17 audit and are unaudited [E16-4].
  • Regional transfer (E17, 64², 3 cases × 20 clusters; “synthetic tracer under ERA5 winds”, not validation). With the box-mean wind, dopt and source_near beat random by 0.35–0.50 (24 h window) and 0.53–0.64 (all past data), 20/20 clusters at k = 8 [E17-1]. They tied in the cool season. In the unseen monsoon regime source_near was ahead (16/20, median −0.06, p = 0.012), but this does not survive Holm correction over 18 tests. Under all past data they tied, so gate-09’s clear D-optimal win did not appear in calm, rotating winds [E17-2]. The 24 h-window forecasts were 1.2–1.4× the persistence bound; all-past-data forecasts beat it about 10× [E17-3].

Alternatives and limits. - E15’s ensembles leave out the test directions, which works against the robust designs. - E16 changes one physical setting at a time, at 32²; its 64² run (about 2,760 CPU-h) was not conducted. - In E17 the 20 clusters of a case share one wind history, so inference over wind histories rests on 3 cases. The solver uses a spatially uniform wind; the within-box wind spread is comparable to the mean. Sources are Nepal-only, and the cool-season boundary layer is far shallower than the model’s. - E18 (observational validation) was not conducted: no usable monitoring data exist near the site [E18-1].

21.7 What remains open

  • Pilot-only experiments. E09, E10 and E12–E16 are 32² pilots with 6 clusters (n = 10 for E16). Their 64² plans are written but were not run.
  • Untested hypotheses. E03’s identifiable-recovery half was never tested. E04’s spectrum-vs-coverage comparison was not run across seeds.
  • Open bugs (gate-12-17):
    • E08 H5 can be NaN when both designs are undefined;
    • E14’s omit-one designs know the omitted source;
    • E13’s field-level rule lacks an oracle condition;
    • E08 and E17 rows lack a true-strength forecast column.
  • Unmeasured calibration. The deployable drifting filter (kf_qsel) was never scored for coverage.
  • Real-world validity is untested. Known source locations, uniform wind, constant or filter-generated emissions and ideal sensors are all assumptions, not findings.
