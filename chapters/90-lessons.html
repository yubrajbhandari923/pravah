21  Lessons and corrections

Every experiment was audited by a separate agent (“gate”), working from the code, tables and run records rather than the author’s summary. The auditors share the authors’ model family, so their independence comes from the task split and from re-deriving numbers. This chapter collects what they caught and what was done about it. Superseded results stay in Git history and in the journals; they are not deleted.

21.1 What these corrections taught us

  • Check the signal-to-noise ratio first. Three experiments were run before the scale problem was acted on; a one-line estimate in the protocol audit had already predicted it.
  • A “steady” control must be exactly steady. The E05 confound came from a realistic-looking jitter in the control.
  • Means of ill-posed estimates are meaningless. Use medians, prior-referenced regularisation, and count failures.
  • Always include a dumb control that knows the same thing. The locations-only control is what revealed that the E06/E07 gains were mostly knowledge of source locations.
  • In-sample information is not held-out performance. Whole-period D on training (and even on test) winds missed the short windows in which a source became invisible.
  • Amendments are fine if recorded. Each change above has a decision file or journal entry with its evidence and the results it affects.
