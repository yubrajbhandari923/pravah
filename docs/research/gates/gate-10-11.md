## Issues, ranked

### 1. (Major, E10 interpretation) The filter's advantage under diurnal truth is a 1 h effect; at 6 h it reverses
Committed `e10-local-paired.csv`, diurnal, `kf_qsel` vs `map_full`:

| k, design | 1 h forecast | 6 h forecast | source RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 dopt | +0.24 [0.06, 0.52], 10/0 | **−0.12 [−0.20, −0.07], 0/10** | −0.04 [−0.31, 0.13] |
| 8 dopt | +0.44 [0.08, 0.57], 11/0 | **−0.13 [−0.21, −0.08], 0/11** | −0.03 [−0.14, 0.14] |
| 8 source_near | +0.55 [0.10, 0.66], 10/0 | **−0.13 [−0.15, −0.09], 0/10** | 0.00 [−0.07, 0.37] |

Every fixed q also loses at 6 h (−0.15 to −0.35, 0/12 to 1/11).

Mechanism (analytic, not a bug): the random-walk forecast persists the current, lagged strength estimate. For a 24 h
sinusoid, persisting a(t − d) has RMS error 2 sin(πd/24) times that of the time-mean. The break-even is at **d = 4 h**,
and a 6 h horizon plus the 2–5 h estimation lag is well beyond it. Under `logrw` (persistence is a good model) the filter
still wins at 6 h (+0.17 to +0.21). The journal/STATUS phrase "random-walk filter wins … for time-varying ones" is
true only at 1 h for diurnal truth.

**Fix:** qualify the wording by horizon and by truth type. If 6–24 h forecasting matters (E11, plan 3), the source model
must carry a periodic or mean-reverting component (e.g. an AR(1) toward a running mean, or a diurnal harmonic state). A
random walk is the wrong prior for a cycle.

### 2. (Major, E10 claim) The calibration result is for an oracle-matched q; the deployable `kf_qsel` was not scored
`score()` computes coverage/CRPS only where `C`/`sd1` exist, and `sel["kf_qsel"]` carries neither. So `cov95_active` is
NaN for `kf_qsel`.

The quoted "0.91–0.98" are fixed q chosen by agreement with the truth's variance:
- diurnal: q = 0.1 gives 0.958–0.976;
- logrw: q = 0.01 gives 0.91–0.96.

Coverage also rewards width:
- q = 1 gives 0.996+;
- under constant truth every q > 0 gives 0.99+ (over-dispersed, not "calibrated").

**Fix:**
- add coverage/CRPS for `kf_qsel` (keep `C`, `sd1` in the selection);
- report CRPS beside coverage;
- reword H3 to "with q near the truth-matched variance, 95 % coverage is 0.91–0.98; the validation-selected filter's
  calibration was not measured".

Separately, H3's map_full lag-1 criterion was never computed (MAP rows have no innovations; `innov_lag1` is NaN).

### 3. (Moderate, E10 framing) The gain is the dynamic source model, not memory length. The claim survives a fair memory competitor
The pilot had fixed MAP windows only. I added three MAP competitors:
- the window chosen per data column on the **same validation-innovation criterion** as `kf_qsel` (6/12/24/72/all);
- exponential-forgetting MAP (λ ∈ {0.995, 0.98, 0.95, 0.9, 0.8}) chosen the same way;
- the test-chosen best (oracle upper bound).

1 h forecast, (competitor − kf_qsel)/competitor, n = 12. "cl" is the 6 (K, seed) clusters (issue 6):

| variant, k, design | vs validation-selected window | vs selected forgetting MAP | vs test-oracle best MAP |
|---|---|---|---|
| diurnal 8 dopt | +0.37 [0.09, 0.49], 12/0, cl 6/6 | +0.35 [0.08, 0.44] | +0.32 [−0.08, 0.41], 9/3 |
| diurnal 8 source_near | +0.42 [0.05, 0.59], 11/0, cl 6/6 | +0.42 [0.04, 0.59] | +0.37 [−0.06, 0.58], 9/3 |
| logrw 8 dopt | +0.30 [0.02, 0.49], 11/1, cl 6/6 | +0.27 [0.07, 0.40] | +0.15 [−0.16, 0.37], 9/3 |
| logrw 8 source_near | +0.32 [0.05, 0.42], 12/0, cl 6/6 | +0.34 [0.19, 0.45] | 0.00 [−0.14, 0.39], 6/6 |

- So "q > 0 beats MAP under time-varying truth" holds against a validation-tuned memory competitor (at 1 h).
- Against the test-oracle MAP the CIs include 0.
- A validation-selected window recovers only +0.01 to +0.10 of map_full's RMSE under diurnal truth (+0.21 to +0.24
  under logrw).

The windowed MAP forecasts F_{t+1}â, i.e. it assumes constant emission since hour 0. The filter tracks the field. Most of
the gain is therefore state tracking under a time-varying source model, not "finite memory". Also note:
- **Perfect model:** the time-varying truths are generated by the filter's own linear recursion. This favours the
  filter over the static-forecast MAPs; state it.
- **Validation-based memory selection is noisy.** Under *constant* truth the validation-selected window is worse than
  map_full (dopt k = 8: −0.14 [−2.38, −0.01], 0/10).
- **`kf_qsel` has a cost under constant truth:** committed paired table, medians 0 to −0.02 but CI lower bounds −0.06
  to −0.48. The regret vs map_full for informative designs is 90th pct +61 %, max +209 % (`e10-local-rows.csv`).
- The lead decision "use `kf_qsel` when sources may vary" should state this cost.

### 4. (Moderate, E10 wording) H1 "error grows monotonically with q" is false for random designs
Committed summary, constant truth, random k = 4, 1 h forecast medians:

| estimator | map_full | q 1e-4 | q 1e-3 | q 1e-2 | q 0.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RMSE | 0.140 | 0.139 | 0.131 | 0.126 | 0.215 |

The data-driven q picks q > 0 in 33/72 random-design const columns. The cause is likely the miscentred N(0, I) prior (true
a ≈ 0.5–5; gate-09 prior moments), which a positive q lets the estimate escape; this is not verified. **Fix:** restrict
the H1 statement to informative designs (dopt, source_near), where it holds (q = 0 test-best in 47/48 cells).

### 5. (Minor, E10) Diurnal source lag: a reasonable claim, but the stated cause and the "no improvement" claim need refining
`e10-local-traces.csv` (mixed K=2 s0, k = 8): the lag maximising correlation with the true a(t) is:

| q | dopt | source_near |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 2–4 h | 2 h |
| 0.01 | 3–4 h | 3 h |
| 1e-3 | 5 h | 4–5 h |

The lag is mostly observability:
- the target a(t) is emitted during t → t+1 and is not yet observed at issue time t;
- transport adds the source-to-sensor travel time (shorter for source_near).

With the 4 h break-even (issue 1), dopt (lag 3–4 h) cannot beat the time-mean, while source_near at q = 0.1 does in this
trace (RMSE 0.74–0.79 vs 1.22–1.44 for map_full). Over 12 scenarios the paired source RMSE is null (median 0 to −0.04). **Fix:** say "the
estimate lags by 2–5 h (one-hour emission timing plus transport), so for a 24 h cycle it is no better than the mean";
use a fixed-lag smoother if source trajectories are an endpoint.

### 6. (Major, statistics, both) n = 12 is 6 independent clusters
For every (K, seed), the veering and mixed scenarios share:
- source layout and strengths;
- noise streams (seeded by replicate);
- **identical test winds** (`scenario-dependence.csv`: 6/6 True).

They differ only in the training winds. The order-statistic CI and the sign test on 12 treat them as independent. On 6
cluster means:
- the smallest attainable two-sided sign-test p is 0.031;
- the n = 6 order-statistic CI is (min, max).

Surviving at cluster level (6/6, p = 0.031):
- E11 fc_w24_rob vs dopt, map24, k = 8 (cluster min +0.069);
- E10 kf_qsel vs selected MAP window (diurnal/logrw, k = 8).

Not surviving:
- E11 fc_w24_rob vs dopt at k = 2, 4 (4/6, p = 0.69);
- most source_near non-inferiority statements.

**Fix:** report cluster-level statistics (or a paired analysis with cluster = (K, seed)). At 64² that is 20 clusters for
40 scenarios.

### 7. (Major, E11) The robust prior's inclusion of the test directions makes the comparison partly in-sample; the dopt result survives, the source_near non-inferiority does not
The 8-direction prior contains two test directions exactly:
- S (180°, the steady southerly at 4 m/s vs 3 m/s);
- N (0°, the "front" of stagnation_then_front).

The diurnal rotation sweeps all directions. I rebuilt the robust designs from independent per-direction block solves with:
- S left out (`noS`);
- N left out (`noN`);
- both left out (`noSN`);
- a non-test direction left out (`noNE`, control).

fc_w24_rob under map24, k = 8 (`summarise.out`):

| prior | vs dopt, all test hours | vs dopt, southerly block | vs source_near, all test hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 directions (pilot) | +0.367 [0.074, 0.576], 12/0, cl 6/6 | +0.416 [0.219, 0.645], 12/0 | −0.015 [−0.043, 0.026] non-inferior |
| no NE (control) | +0.367 [0.194, 0.528], 12/0 | +0.416 [0.156, 0.680] | +0.015 [−0.054, 0.071] |
| no N | +0.298 [0.054, 0.576], 12/0 | +0.423 [0.240, 0.630] | −0.063 [−0.142, −0.015] **worse** |
| **no S** | **+0.215 [0.029, 0.576], 11/1**, cl 5/6 | +0.236 [0.057, 0.613], 10/2 | −0.042 [−0.116, 0.033] unresolved |
| **no S, no N** | **+0.255 [0.071, 0.576], 10/2**, cl 5/6 | +0.306 [0.160, 0.668] | −0.035 [−0.090, 0.012] unresolved |

- Dropping S or N changes the k = 8 sites in 72–78 % of (scenario, k) cases, and dropping both in 100 %.
- The design is not stable to the prior, but the advantage over dopt is.

Interpretation:
- About 40 % of the median gain over dopt (0.37 → 0.22) comes from having the test's own direction in the prior. The
  rest is generic multi-direction robustness.
- Non-inferiority to source_near at the 5 % margin is **lost** without the test directions.
- At k = 2 and 4 none of the variants beats dopt with a CI excluding 0. The exception is noS at k = 4, +0.30
  [0.065, 0.578], which is an unstable small-sample result.
- Under map_grow, fc_w24_rob is worse than or equal to dopt under every prior.

**Defensible wording:** "Under the 24 h estimator at k = 8, a design that averages forecast variance over steady 24 h
wind directions beats D-optimal training design (+0.37 with an 8-direction prior; +0.22 to +0.26 when the test's
directions are excluded from the prior). It is comparable to source_near only when the prior includes the test
directions. It is worse than dopt under the growing-window estimator."

### 8. (Moderate, E11) Greedy + 1-swap is a local optimum, sometimes far from the k = 2 optimum
Exhaustive k = 2 search over ~17 500 feasible pairs per scenario (`e11-bruteforce-k2.csv`):
- greedy + swap is globally optimal in 36/48 (scenario, objective) cases (all K = 2 cases);
- it misses in 12/48, all at K = 4:

| objective | misses (K = 4) | objective-value gap |
|---|---|---|
| fc_grow_rob | 4/6 | up to 2.2× (mixed K4 s2: −0.00204 vs −0.00091) |
| fc_w24_rob | 4/6 | ≈ 1.5–1.8 % |
| fc_grow | 1/6 | 28 % |
| fc_w24 | 1/6 | 3.8 % |

The code is correct (the tiny-case brute-force test passes); the heuristic is the limit (as gate-06 F3). The pilot
statement "non-robust forecast objectives mostly reproduce dopt" is partly a statement about the optimiser.

**Fix:**
- report k = 2 optimality gaps;
- add a multi-start or 2-swap refinement for the forecast objectives, or state "local optimum of the objective" wherever
  designs are compared.

### 9. (Moderate, E11) Predicted-vs-realised discrepancy is the prior's miscentring, confirmed
The data-free prediction assumes a ~ N(0, I). The truth is LogUniform(0.5, 5) a_ref, so E[a²] ≈ 5.4, not 1. The error
of the MAP is −Σ a + Σ Aᵀe. Its exact expected MSE is aᵀΣQΣa + tr(QΣMΣ), which reduces to tr(QΣ) only when E[aaᵀ] = I.
With the **true** a (diagnostic only), realised/predicted 1 h RMSE for non-random designs is:

| estimator | N(0, I) prediction (pilot): median, 90th pct, max | bias-aware with true a: median, 90th pct, range |
|---|---|---|
| map24 | 0.975, 1.86, 2.85 | 0.966, 1.02, [0.83, 1.15] |
| map_grow | 0.969, 1.17 | 0.969, 1.16 |

- **map24:** the bias-aware prediction removes the discrepancy.
- **map_grow:** the remaining spread is sampling, consistent with K-dimensional errors that are nearly constant across
  issue times, averaged over 5 draws (inferred, not separately tested).
- The pilot's 90th percentile of 2.7 is dominated by random networks (360 of 612 rows).

Implication: the design objective **weights weakly informed networks' bias as if a had unit second moment**. That
under-penalises networks that leave a source poorly observed. A design may legitimately use the declared strength prior
(`DesignInput.prior_loguniform`): E[aaᵀ] = s²I + ā²11ᵀ (ā = 1.954, s² = 1.555, gate-09).

## What must change before the E11 64² run (stage 2 on cached E07 fields + robust-prior solves)

1. **Solve the design prior per direction, not as one 8-block calendar.** Use 8 independent 24 h blocks, each with its
   own short spin-up. They give identical designs at 32², and any subset prior then costs nothing.
   - Pre-declare a leave-test-directions-out prior (`noSN`: drop 0° and 180°) as a co-reported robust variant, labelled
     as the out-of-sample check. Alternatively use a direction grid offset by 22.5° that contains no test direction.
   - Cost: 8 × (24 h + spin-up). With a 48 h spin-up that is 576 h per source (≈ 2.4× the planned 240 h, ≈ 60 CPU-h).
     With the 12 h spin-up that sufficed at 32², it is ≈ 288 h (≈ 30 CPU-h). Verify the spin-up choice on one source
     first.
2. **Cluster-aware statistics:** cluster = (K, seed), 20 clusters. Use these for the exploratory Holm family.
3. **Fix the predicted-RMSE descriptive** to use the declared strength prior's second moment E[aaᵀ] (and the true a as
   a diagnostic column), so the predcheck tests the transport/noise model, not the prior's centring. Keep the
   preregistered N(0, I) objective unchanged for the main designs. If a bias-aware objective is added, label it a
   post hoc deviation.
4. **Optimiser:** record greedy and refined values and add a cheap multi-start (e.g. swap-refine from the dopt and
   source_near sets as well, keeping the best by the objective). Otherwise state "local optimum" in every comparison.
5. **Report claims per k with cluster CIs.** No verdict on the pooled-k row.
6. Unchanged from gate-09: at 64² VanLeer is ≈ 1 % non-linear. The predicted-vs-realised check there also absorbs
   superposition error; record that rather than read it as a prior effect.
