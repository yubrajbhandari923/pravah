# PRAVAH peer presentation: where should the sensors go, and what can they tell us?

Final version, 2026-10-15. Companion to `docs/FINAL_RESEARCH_REPORT.md` (section numbers below refer to it; every number there carries its finding / run / path reference). All results are **synthetic**: real Belauri geography and a modelled WorldPop raster are inputs, but emissions, atmosphere and sensor readings are generated by the model. No measured data, no empirical validation. Only F-051 (numerics) is independently reviewed in its final wording; every other current finding is *preliminary*. Figures: `docs/figures/final/`.

Suggested length: 25 minutes plus questions, about 14 slides (one per section below, with its figure).

---

## 0. The one-minute story

We built a cheap, verified, time-dependent source-inversion testbed on a finite-volume advection-diffusion model, and asked what a handful of fixed sensors can do for population-weighted air-quality reconstruction. In the synthetic setting: a causal time-varying estimator beats a causal static fit by a lot, but because emissions change, not for the reason we guessed; temporal regularisation helps only a little, only with few sensors, and not across winds; surrogate-designed layouts beat random layouts, in the toy and at Belauri scale, but a static design does as well and the toy's "go where people live" heuristic is not beaten; local-versus-boundary attribution fails through non-detection, not mislabelling; wind-direction error swamps everything (at 10-20 degrees the time-varying estimator stops helping at Belauri scale). The design objective is statistically fragile: six design scenarios are not enough, the cheap proxy cannot certify more, the mesh test is borderline (500 m shape error probably 5-10 %), and population weighting at Belauri scale is "not resolved" with 12 test cases. **Our own controls and independent reviews overturned or corrected most first-pass headlines; that record is part of the result (Section 12).**

## 1. Can we trust the numerics? (Q1)

**Claim.** The dynamic forward model is verified (25 of 25 pre-set checks): mass closes to about 1e-15, superposition holds to 1e-15, first-order temporal convergence (order 0.997), and a boundary basis switches face exactly when the wind reverses [F-051; Report 2.3]. **Caveat:** the scheme is numerically diffusive, K_eff/K between 2.4 and 9.4 over the winds used, so "K = 100 m^2/s" is not the diffusivity of the simulated plume [Report 2.4]. Van Leer, Belauri-scale dynamics and spatial order beyond monotone convergence are **not** verified.

**Figure 0** (`fig00_dyn00_verification.png`). *What it shows.* Left: the mass-closure and conservation residuals for ten checks, all passing at about 1e-15 to 1e-14 against tolerances several orders larger. Right: the temporal-order and refinement checks, with the closed-box temporal order at about 1.0 (first order) and the two refinement checks passing. It says the machinery is internally consistent on a toy box; it says nothing about physical realism.

**Figure 1** (`fig01_concentration_fields.png`). *What it shows.* Top: the true concentration field of one toy episode at 1, 3 and 5 h; three sources, four sensors (white squares), three synthetic settlements (white contours), plumes carried across the domain by a reversal wind. Bottom: stored Belauri forward fields (synthetic WSW wind, 500 m mesh), combined and external-only. Orientation only: the sensors see a small, wind-dependent fraction of the field. A model output, not a result.

## 2. Does temporal regularisation help? (Q2)

**Claim.** With 4 sensors a TV-regularised nonnegative inversion (M2) lowers population-weighted RMSE by 15.7 % (2 % noise) and 20.1 % (10 %) against a fair independent-bin baseline; at 9 sensors the gain is 0.7 % / 2.4 % [F-055; Report 6.2]. **What failed:** one wind (rotation_a) supplies 68 % / 48 % of the effect; without it the gain at 2 % noise is 6.8 %, below our own 10 % criterion; a t-interval over the six wind means includes zero. Real on these six winds, not shown to generalise.

**Figure 10** (`fig10_dyn02_by_wind.png`). *What it shows.* Mean M2-minus-M1 error per wind at 4 sensors (negative = TV helps; bars are episode standard errors). rotation_a and steady_b carry nearly all of it; steady_a, rotation_b, reversal and slow_reversal sit near zero. The headline percentage hides a modest, wind-specific improvement.

## 3. What does a causal time-varying estimator add, and why? (Q3)

**Claim.** A causal time-varying estimator (CAUSAL) lowers error against a causal *static* fit (called CT) by 57 % / 54 % (4 sensors, 2 % / 10 % noise) and 79 % / 77 % (9 sensors), and misses 9.7 % of source changes against 61 % for CT [F-065; Report 6.3]. **Read it correctly:** CT is the causal running static fit, so the contrast is time-varying versus time-invariant model, and the gain comes from tracking emission changes (about zero on constant-emission episodes; CAUSAL is worse on activations at 4 sensors).

**What failed.** (a) The registered negative control (steady winds should gain less) would fire again: all 8 steady-wind intervals exclude zero; it was retired by decision D-024 before the rerun. (b) The proposed "plume carry-over" mechanism is untested and unsupported. (c) The relative wind-variation prediction holds in direction in 8 of 8 comparisons but every interval includes zero and it was formulated after seeing the first run: undetermined. (d) The first version of this analysis (not causal; flawed detection rule) was withdrawn after review (Section 12).

**Figure 4** (`fig04_dyn03_forest.png`). *What it shows.* Left: the 12 paired contrasts with 98.75 % CIs; every interval sits far left of zero, but the three estimator pairs answer different questions. Right: CAUSAL-minus-CT split into steady (grey) and variable (red) winds; the intervals overlap heavily, which is the control that would fire. (The panel title says "FAILS"; under the final protocol the control is retired and "would fire" is the right wording.)

**Figure 3** (`fig03_history_reconstruction.png`). *What it shows.* One median-gain "activation" episode (re-simulated, chosen by a rule). The true S1 emission jumps at hour 2. The static fit (labelled CT; the legend wording predates the correction) lags far below the truth for hours; the causal time-varying estimate jumps at the right bin; the retrospective smoother is closest because it also uses future data. The right panel checks the re-simulation against the stored error values. A mechanism illustration, not evidence.

## 4. Do designed layouts beat natural baselines? (Q4)

**Toy (DYN-04).** Layouts designed on a time-resolved population-weighted surrogate beat random layouts in all 8 budget x noise cells (-32 % to -84 %) and spread layouts at k >= 3 (-64 % to -72 %); of 40 contrasts 23 are robust wins and 1 a robust loss under both multiplicity families, 11 ties [F-061; Report 6.4]. **What failed:** against the high-population heuristic the designed layout is not better at k = 3 and marginally worse at k = 5; spread beats it at k = 1 / 10 % noise (+20 %); the surrogate ranks layouts well only at larger budgets (Spearman 0.43 at k = 1, 0.89-0.95 at k = 5).

**Belauri scale (DYN-08 v2, Section 9).** Surrogate-designed layouts beat random layouts in 4 of 4 cells (-23 % to -32 %; one borderline) but are indistinguishable from a static-model-optimal layout (0 of 4), so we can say "surrogate-designed beats random", not "dynamic design beats static design".

**Figure 2** (`fig02_sensor_layouts.png`). *What it shows.* The DYN-04 layouts at k = 2, 3, 5 over the synthetic population: dynamic-optimal, high-population, spread, static-optimal and source-optimal sites. The high-population and dynamic layouts often neighbour each other near the settlements, which is why they tie; source-optimal sits near the sources.

**Figure 5** (`fig05_dyn04_forest.png`). *What it shows.* All 40 dynamic-optimal versus baseline contrasts at k = 1, 2, 3, 5 (rows are baseline and noise level). Blue: effect criterion met; grey: tie; orange: baseline better; grey band is the 10 % minimum effect. The blue block against random and spread is the success; the grey and orange points in the high-population, static and source rows are the failures.

## 5. How many design scenarios are enough, and can a proxy be trusted? (Q5, Q-013)

**Claim.** For the closed-form surrogate the registered "90th-percentile regret <= 5 %" criterion is met from N = 24 (noise 0.10, k = 2) and N = 48 (noise 0.02, k = 3) but **not met up to N = 192** in the other two cells. At N <= 12 an ensemble that misses one wind yields a layout about 30x worse than the optimum [F-062; Report 6.5]. Under the real constrained estimator, more design episodes are **not shown to help** (N = 6 versus N = 192 intervals include zero in all cells), and the cell the surrogate certifies costs +28 % [8, 54] in the real estimator. An unconstrained-ridge surrogate cannot certify sufficiency for the constrained estimator.

**Q-013 (is the design objective identifiable with 6 design cases?):** partly addressed, **not closed**. Toy proxy: no, 6 is insufficient. Belauri greedy objective [F-072, F-070]: greedy designs are 28-79 % above the best found and design-sample optimism is large, but all EXP-026 draws share the same 6 design cases, so the replicate spread was never measured. EXP-027b and DYN-08 do not answer it either. Open: how many design cases Belauri needs.

**Figure 6** (`fig06_dyn05_sufficiency.png`). *What it shows.* Top: the surrogate's 90th-percentile (blue) and mean (orange) relative regret against the number of design episodes N, one panel per (noise, k), with the 5 % line (log axes). Two panels cross the line and stay there; the other two never do, and the noise 0.10, k = 3 panel dips at N = 96 then rises. Bottom: the excess error of the same ensembles under the real estimator: positive with wide intervals and no steady fall. The proxy and the real estimator disagree about where design is stable.

## 6. Can local changes be told apart from transboundary influx? (Q6)

**Claim.** Where a change is detected, attribution is right: pooled wrong-group rate at d = 16 with 4 sensors is 0.00-0.02 for five scenarios; at d = 8 it is <= 0.08. The real 4-sensor limitation is **non-detection**: mean detection at d = 16 is 0.58-0.94 (S1: 0.58, clearly detected in only 4 of 6 winds) against 0.79-1.00 with 9 sensors. A boundary-reference sensor gives no benefit [F-063; Report 6.6]. Some events are invisible by construction (after a reversal the "influx ends" basis has no inflow face).

**What failed / was contradicted.** P3 (>= 25 % misattribution at d = 2) is contradicted where evaluable (0.05-0.11) and mostly not evaluable. The first headline ("17-26 % misattribution at d = 16") was an averaging artefact and was withdrawn; the flip of P2 to "holds" follows a scoring rule adopted after the first verdict (old metric: S1/L4 at d = 16 is 0.31). No confidence intervals on these rates were stored.

**Figure 7** (`fig07_dyn06_detection_misattribution.png`). *What it shows.* Columns: 4 sensors, 9 sensors, 4 sensors with an upwind-boundary reference. Top: mean detection against distinguishability d (noise standard deviations), by scenario; detection climbs slowly at 4 sensors (S1 reaches 0.58) and to 0.8-1.0 at 9. Bottom: pooled wrong-group rate where detection is clearly above the false-alarm level; it falls to about 0-0.04 at d = 16 in all layouts, with S5 at 4 sensors starting high (0.62 at d = 2). Missing points at small d mean no wind was evaluable.

## 7. What does model error do? (Q7)

**Claim.** A 5 / 10 / 20 degree wind-direction error multiplies the 4-sensor error by 1.84 / 2.59 / 3.74 and the 9-sensor error by 2.72 / 4.51 / 7.33; the TV advantage at 4 sensors erodes from -20 % to -3 %; beyond about 15-20 degrees the 9-sensor layout loses its absolute advantage [F-056; Report 6.7]. At Belauri scale (DYN-08 v2, Figure 15 below) the advantage of M2 over M1 does not merely erode, it **reverses** at 10-20 degrees [F-076]. **Withdrawn:** "more sensors tighten the fit to a wrong model" (the extra-source test is confounded with a sensor location).

**Figure 8** (`fig08_dyn07_model_error.png`). *What it shows.* Left: error multiplier against rotation for 4 and 9 sensors and both estimators (the 9-sensor curves rise faster). Middle: absolute error, where the 4- and 9-sensor curves cross between 10 and 20 degrees. Right: the M2-versus-M1 advantage under each perturbation: about -20 % with the right model, near zero for 9 sensors, about -3 % at 20 degrees. Hyper-parameters were deliberately not re-tuned per condition.

## 8. Is the mesh safe for sensor decisions? (Q8)

**EXP-027 (void) [F-057].** A deliberately coarse 2 km control passed the test it was registered to fail: layouts from independent 6-case design draws differ by 3-4 km, more than degrading the mesh 8x. A statement about the test's power, not about the mesh.

**EXP-027b (the decisive version) [F-075].** Same cases on every mesh (72 cases, 3 draws x 24, seeds 1201-1203), 250 m reference, 500 m main, 2 km control. **The 2 km control passed its gate in 4 of 4 cells, so the test is not void.** By the registered rule the 500 m mesh is **INADEQUATE (E_lo95 > 0.05 in 2 of 4 cells)**, but: it rests on one cell (0.0595 at noise 0.05 / budget 3); the other cell clears the margin by 0.0016, within bootstrap noise; the percentile interval of this statistic is biased toward "inadequate" (a basic interval makes both cells inconclusive); the 0.05 margin is a convention. Best reading: 500 m shape error is **probably 5-10 %**. Layout rankings are largely preserved (rho 0.94-0.96); decision equivalence is neither shown nor excluded. 500 m stays provisional. **[PENDING: a post-hoc sensitivity analysis of EXP-027b (per-draw, leave-one-draw-out, alternative intervals, menu-restricted) is being prepared; the script has been written but the job has not been run, and no result from it is reported here.]**

**Figure 9** (`fig09_exp027_void.png`). *What it shows.* For four (budget, noise) cells, the layout displacement between the 250 m reference and the 500 m design (blue), the 2 km control (orange) and between replicate design draws (grey). The orange and blue boxes overlap the grey one: mesh degradation cannot be distinguished from design noise, so the first control could not do its job.

**Figure 11** (`wrapup_exp027b_adequacy.png`). *What it shows.* Left: E (mesh-induced discrepancy) with 95 % intervals for the 500 m mesh (blue) and the 2 km control (orange), stage 1 hollow and stage 2 filled, against the 0.05 margin. The control sits far above the margin in all four stage-2 cells (that is why the test can fail), while the blue intervals straddle or only just clear it; the printed labels are INADEQUATE, INCONCLUSIVE, INADEQUATE, INCONCLUSIVE. Middle and right: median J ratio and rank correlation, no intervals stored; rho is about 0.94-0.96 at 500 m and far lower for the control.

## 9. Does any of this transfer to Belauri? (Q9; DYN-08)

**v1 (RUN-0099) stopped at its own registered stop rules; no verdict [F-069].** The two registered controls failed: a "low-population" layout did not lose to spread in 3 of 4 cells, and two groups of random layouts differed at k = 5. The review showed the controls were badly constructed (not a pipeline bug). Descriptive only: dyn_opt below all 10 random layouts in all 4 cells (-23 % to -32 %).

**v2 (RUN-0101) is a post-hoc-corrected rerun, not an independent replication [F-076].** New controls (a worst-by-construction layout; fresh random groups with a layout x episode bootstrap), fixed bin-0 handling, model-error CIs, fresh episodes; the winds, dyn_opt layouts and 10 random layouts are the same as v1. Controls passed. dyn_opt beats the 10 random layouts in 4 of 4 cells (-31.5 %, -27.4 %, -23.3 %, -31.5 %; k = 5 / 2 % borderline and not robust: against 20 never-seen random layouts it is -15.6 % and not met). Against single layouts: spread 3 of 4, high-population 2 of 4, source-optimal 1 of 4, **static-optimal 0 of 4**. M2 beats M1 in 8 of 8 rows (-24 % to -52 %). Under wind rotation the M2 error grows 3.6 / 7 / 13 times (k = 3 / 2 %, spread, 5 / 10 / 20 degrees) and **M2's advantage over M1 reverses** in 4 of 8 rows at 10 degrees and 6 of 8 at 20. **H8 is "supported under the D-026 counting rule, with post-hoc amendments"; confidence low; not a clean confirmatory test.** The mesh error inflation (x1.26-2.54) is as large as the design effects (24-32 %).

**Figure 12** (`wrapup_dyn08_v1_v2_controls.png`). *What it shows.* A table-figure of control outcomes per (k, noise) cell. v1 columns (lowpop versus spread; random groups 0-4 versus 5-9) show FAIL entries in red: three lowpop checks and the random-groups check at both k = 5 cells (-20 %, -17 %). v2 columns (spread versus worst layout; random group A versus B) all pass. Shown unedited; this is why v2 exists.

**Figure 13** (`wrapup_dyn08v2_controls.png`). *What it shows.* The v2 controls with intervals: the worst-layout control (blue) is clearly below zero in all four cells; the random-group control (orange) is centred near zero with wide intervals; the v1 low-population layout (grey, not gating) is indistinguishable from spread on the v2 episodes.

**Figure 14** (`wrapup_dyn08v2_forest.png`). *What it shows.* dyn_opt minus comparison in M2 error for each cell. Blue: against random layouts (two-level bootstrap), -23 % to -31 %. Grey ("this layout only"): clear wins over spread and, at k = 3, over high-population; static-optimal and source-optimal sit near zero with intervals spanning zero in most cells. The picture behind "surrogate-designed beats random; static design is indistinguishable".

**Figure 15** (`wrapup_dyn08v2_model_error.png`). *What it shows.* Top: M2 error relative to the matched model (log axis) for truth rotated 0 / 5 / 10 / 20 degrees, speed x1.2 and a 250 m truth mesh, dyn_opt (blue) against spread (orange): steep growth with rotation, and the two layouts track each other. ## 10. Does population-weighted design help at Belauri scale? (Q10; EXP-026)

**Result [F-070, F-071, F-072].** 12 held-out synthetic scenarios, budgets 2-8, noise 0.05 / 0.15. H1 (population weighting lowers population-weighted RMSE) is **not resolved and optimiser-dependent**: exact/best-found layouts give 0 of 200 cells beneficial and 5 harmful (only 2 distinct comparisons, at cells where the shared area reference layout ranks 2nd-3rd of about 50); greedy-versus-greedy gives 24 beneficial and 5 harmful. **Power is low** (12 cases detect about a third of the baseline RMSE; benefits of 20 % or more are not excluded in 15 of 35 cells): absence of evidence. The WorldPop fixture is a joint null. Greedy designs are 28-79 % above the best found; all draws share the same 6 design cases. One shared deviation: the Phase 2 regression check was skipped (D-028) after the first run (RUN-0097) stopped on it.

**Figure 17** (`exp026_fig1_pop_rmse_effect.png`). *What it shows.* The population layout minus the area layout in test population RMSE (% of area RMSE), by budget, for the two noise levels: blue dots are concentrated synthetic fixtures (mean of 6 draws, mostly within about +-20 %, a few at +45 to +50 %), orange diamonds WorldPop with very wide 95 % intervals. No systematic gain; the wide intervals show the low power.

**Figure 18** (`exp026_fig2_tradeoff_plane.png`). *What it shows.* Population-weighted versus area-weighted RMSE change per comparison: the cloud runs along the diagonal (both worse, or both better), with few points in the true trade-off quadrant.

**Figure 19** (`exp026_fig3_greedy_gap_optimism.png`). *What it shows.* Left: greedy design objective 30-79 % above the best found at every budget. Right: design-split optimism (other-half minus own-half objective, 3-case halves, budgets 5 and 8) of about 175-530 %. The design objective, not the test set, is the weak link.

## 11. What failed (an honest slide)

* **DYN-03 negative control** would fire (steady winds gain as much); retired by D-024; the "carry-over" explanation is untested [F-065].
* **DYN-05 H4**: more design episodes do not stabilise the objective in 2 of 4 cells; for the real estimator they are not shown to help [F-062].
* **EXP-027** void (the control passed a test it should fail) [F-057]; **EXP-027b** borderline INADEQUATE with a biased interval, resting on one cell [F-075].
* **DYN-04**: the designed layout does not beat the high-population heuristic in the toy at k >= 3, loses to spread at k = 1 / 10 % noise [F-061].
* **DYN-02**: the TV gain does not generalise over winds [F-055]. **DYN-06**: P3 contradicted [F-063].
* **DYN-08 v1**: both registered controls failed; the run stopped without a verdict [F-069]. **v2**: a dynamic-versus-static benefit did not appear (0 of 4); a registered sub-prediction about the high-population margin failed; the TV advantage reverses under 10-20 degrees of wind error [F-076].
* **EXP-026**: no robust benefit from population weighting, and low power means we cannot say there is none [F-070, F-072].
* **Not done:** empirical validation; a mesh finer than 500 m; terrain-coupled flow; a direct measurement of design-objective spread (Q-013).

## 12. What our controls and reviews changed (Report "Corrections log")

Six kinds of error: a control or baseline that did not test what it was meant to; a statistic dominated by a nuisance; multiplicity or reference choices that flattered a result; interpretation beyond the evidence; arithmetic slips; low power presented as a null. Examples: DYN-03's first "causal" estimators peeked at the future and its "current-time" baseline was a static fit (F-053 -> F-064 -> F-065); DYN-06's "17-26 % misattribution" was a false-alarm artefact (F-044 -> F-060 -> F-063); DYN-05's reference layout was itself poor (F-046 -> F-058 -> F-062); DYN-04's wins depended on the multiplicity family (F-050 -> F-059 -> F-061); the cost of the decisive mesh test was understated (F-048 -> F-057); EXP-026's "no benefit" was a power- and optimiser-dependent non-result (F-066..68 -> F-070..72); EXP-027b's verdict used a biased interval (F-073 -> F-075); DYN-08 v1's controls were bad and v2 is a labelled post-hoc rerun (F-069; F-074 -> F-076). Superseded ids are cited nowhere else.

## 13. What is established, what is not

* **Established (reviewed):** verified numerics (F-051); matched recovery, greedy vs spread from 3 sensors, wind error dominating discretisation mismatch, bit-identical solver routing (F-002, F-006, F-007, F-009).
* **Supported synthetic, preliminary:** causal time-varying > causal static fit; surrogate-designed > random layouts (toy and Belauri); attribution right where detected, non-detection limits 4 sensors; wind error costs a factor 1.7-9; TV gain small and wind-concentrated.
* **Not resolved:** mesh adequacy (about 5-10 % shape error); population weighting at Belauri scale; Q-013; whether more design episodes help the real estimator.
* **Contradicted:** DYN-03 negative-control logic; DYN-06 P3; DYN-05 H4 in 2 of 4 cells; dynamic-optimal beating high-population at k >= 3 in the toy.
* **Speculative:** carry-over as the mechanism; dynamic over static design; transfer below 500 m or with terrain-coupled flow; anything about real air quality.

## 14. Positioning and next steps

**Positioning (conservative).** Time-varying emission inversion, goal-oriented and population-weighted design, TV regularisation, boundary bias in regional inversions and sample-average design are all established (Sharrock & Kantas 2022; Attia et al. 2018; Wu-Chen-Ghattas 2023; Henriquez et al. 2015; Tibshirani et al. 2005; Lauvaux et al. 2012; Bhardwaj & Subramanian 2026 preprint). We claim **no novel method**. Defensible contributions are empirical and methodological: the structure-aware account of local-versus-boundary separability with non-detection as the real limit; the proxy-versus-real-estimator disagreement on design sufficiency; the verified cheap response framework; and the record of controls and reviews that changed our headlines. A systematic literature search remains to be done (Report 10).

**Next research directions (Report 12).**
1. A 250 m or finer mesh before any further Belauri claim; finish the EXP-027b sensitivity analysis first.
2. Larger, wind-stratified design ensembles; measure the replicate spread of the Belauri design objective (Q-013).
3. Power analysis with more than 12 test cases for population weighting; several reference layouts; report greedy and best-found.
4. A registered test of the history mechanism (vary plume persistence at fixed wind; a non-degenerate short-window baseline).
5. A constrained-estimator-aware design objective; a registered dynamic-versus-static test with enough layouts.
6. Wind-uncertain inversion; terrain-coupled flow.
7. Empirical station and low-cost sensor data for hindcast validation; then the Nepal roadmap (verified boundary and trajectories, verified population raster, independent review of every finding).

## Likely questions (prepared answers)

* *"Is a 16-20 % TV gain worth it?"* On six winds, 4 sensors, matched model, concentrated in rotating winds; gone at 9 sensors and reversed under 10-20 degrees of wind error at Belauri scale. Not claimed beyond that.
* *"Does 'history helps' hold?"* The contrast is time-varying versus a causal static fit; the gain is from tracking emission changes; carry-over was not tested.
* *"Why not trust the surrogate?"* It ignores nonnegativity and TV; Spearman 0.43 at k = 1; it certifies stability in a cell where the real estimator shows +28 % error.
* *"Is 500 m adequate?"* Rankings are preserved (rho 0.94-0.96) but the registered rule says inadequate (borderline, biased interval); treat it as provisional.
* *"Did DYN-08 confirm the toy?"* Partly, and not cleanly: v2 was a post-hoc-corrected rerun; the surrogate-designed layouts beat random ones, a static design does as well, and wind error removes the TV advantage.
* *"What about real data?"* None used. This is a methods testbed.
