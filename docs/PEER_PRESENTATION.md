# PRAVAH peer presentation: where should the sensors go, and what can they tell us?

DRAFT 1. Companion to `docs/FINAL_RESEARCH_REPORT.md` (section numbers below refer to it; every number there carries its finding / run / path reference). All results are **synthetic**: real Belauri geography is an input, but emissions, winds and readings are generated by the model. No measured data, no empirical validation. All current findings except F-051 are *preliminary* (predecessors reviewed, final wording not). Figures: `docs/figures/final/`.

Suggested length: 20-25 minutes plus questions, 12 slides (one per section below, with the figure).

---

## 0. The one-minute story

We built a cheap, verified, time-dependent source-inversion testbed on a finite-volume advection-diffusion model, and used it to ask what a handful of fixed sensors can do for population-weighted air-quality reconstruction. In the synthetic setting: history helps a lot (but mostly for a boring reason); temporal regularisation helps only a little and only with few sensors; informed layouts beat random ones but **not** the simple "put sensors where people live" heuristic; local-versus-boundary attribution fails because of non-detection, not mislabelling; and model error (wind direction) swamps everything. The design objective itself is statistically fragile: six design scenarios are not enough, the cheap proxy cannot certify more, and our mesh-stability experiment was **void**. Belauri (DYN-08), the decisive mesh test (EXP-027b) and the objective-identifiability question (Q-013) are pending.

## 1. Can we trust the numerics? (Q1)

**Claim.** The dynamic forward model is verified (25/25 pre-set checks): mass closes to ~1e-15, superposition holds to 1e-15, first-order temporal convergence (order 0.997), and a boundary basis switches face exactly when the wind reverses [F-051; Report §2.3]. **Caveat that matters for interpretation:** the scheme is numerically diffusive, K_eff/K between 2.4 and 9.4 over the Phase 2 winds (6.3 at 500 m in the convergence study), so "K = 100 m^2/s" is not the diffusivity of the simulated plume [Report §2.4]. Van Leer, Belauri-scale dynamics and spatial order beyond monotone convergence are **not** verified.

**Figure 0** (`fig00_dyn00_verification.png`) and **Figure 1** (`fig01_concentration_fields.png`).

*What Figure 1 shows.* Top row: the true concentration field of one toy episode at 1, 3 and 5 h. Source S1 switches on at 2 h; a reversal wind carries three plumes across the domain; white squares are the four sensors, white contours the synthetic settlements. Bottom row: stored Belauri forward fields (WSW wind, 500 m mesh) showing combined and external-only plumes. The point of the figure is orientation: sensors see a small, wind-dependent fraction of the field, and a settlement can sit in a plume no sensor touches. It is a model output, not a result.

## 2. Does temporal regularisation help reconstruct changing sources? (Q2)

**Claim.** With 4 sensors, a TV-regularised nonnegative inversion (M2) lowers population-weighted RMSE by 15.7 % (2 % noise; -0.00051 [-0.00107, -0.00009] ug/m^3) and 20.1 % (10 %; -0.00067 [-0.00122, -0.00022]) against a *fair* independent-bin baseline. With 9 sensors the gain is 0.7 % / 2.4 %: nothing left to gain, the estimator is within 9-17 % of the representation floor [F-055; Report §6.2].

**What failed.** The effect is concentrated: one wind (rotation_a) supplies 68 % (2 %) / 48 % (10 %) of it; without it the gain is 6.8 % at 2 % noise (below our own 10 % criterion). A t-interval over the six wind means includes zero. So the effect is real *on these six winds*, not shown to generalise. Earlier "24-25 %" and "30-45 %" results were solver and baseline artefacts and are withdrawn.

**Figure 10** (`fig10_dyn02_by_wind.png`).

*What Figure 10 shows.* Mean M2 minus M1 error per wind (4 sensors, 16 episodes per wind, with s.e. bars). Negative means TV helps. Rotation_a and steady_b carry nearly all of it; steady_a, slow_reversal and reversal sit at zero (reversal very slightly positive). This is the honest picture behind the headline percentage: a modest, wind-specific improvement.

## 3. Does measurement history help, and why? (Q3)

**Claim.** A causal filter lowers error relative to a current-time-only estimator by 57 % / 54 % (4 sensors, 2 % / 10 % noise; -0.00798 [-0.01056, -0.00544]) and 79 % / 77 % (9 sensors); it misses 9.7 % of source changes (4 sensors) versus 61 % for the current-time-only estimator, with a 0.4-0.5 bin (about 25-30 min) detection delay [F-064; Report §6.3]. All 12 registered contrasts pass.

**What failed (a registered prediction).** We predicted history would help *more under varying wind*, with steady winds as the negative control. The control failed: all eight steady-wind intervals exclude zero. Weakly larger on variable winds at 4 sensors, not at 9. Our best reading is plume carry-over (earlier emissions are still in later observations), **a mechanism we did not test**. The control was retired after seeing the result (disclosed). Also: the current-time-only baseline is deliberately naive, so most of the contrast measures the cost of ignoring carry-over. This version (v2) replaces a first analysis in which the "causal" estimators used a non-causal prior; a reviewer caught that.

**Figure 4** (`fig04_dyn03_forest.png`) and **Figure 3** (`fig03_history_reconstruction.png`).

*What Figure 4 shows.* Left: the 12 paired contrasts with 98.75 % CIs; every interval is far from zero, but note the three estimator pairs answer different questions (naive vs causal; non-causal vs causal; independent bins vs causal). Right: the same CAUSAL-minus-CT contrast split into steady (grey) and variable (red) winds: the grey intervals sit where the red ones do, which is the failed negative control.

*What Figure 3 shows.* One median-gain episode (re-simulated; J_pop matches the stored value to 1e-15). The true S1 emission jumps at hour 2. The current-time-only estimate (orange) lags and stays low for hours because it ignores the emission already sensed; the causal filter (blue) catches the jump in the right bin; the retrospective smoother (green) is closest because it also sees the future. An illustration of the mechanism, not evidence.

## 4. Do designed layouts beat natural baselines? (Q4)

**Claim.** Layouts designed on a time-resolved population-weighted surrogate beat random layouts in all 8 budget x noise cells (-32 % to -84 %) and spread layouts at k >= 3 (-64 % to -72 %); of 40 contrasts, 23 are robust wins and 1 a robust loss under both multiplicity families, 11 ties [F-061; Report §6.4].

**What failed.** Against the **high-population heuristic** the designed layout is not better at k = 3 (+23 % [-4, +49] at 2 % noise) and is marginally *worse* at k = 5 (+13 % [+2, +25]); spread beats it at k = 1/10 % noise (+20 % [+11, +29]); source-optimal beats it at k = 2/10 % noise (+35 %, lenient family only). With three concentrated settlements, "go where people live" is a strong baseline; our fixture flatters it. The surrogate ranks layouts well only at larger budgets (Spearman 0.43 at k = 1, 0.89-0.95 at k = 5), and greedy is 3.1x the exhaustive optimum at k = 3. Evaluation hyper-parameters were tuned on the spread layout (a comparator).

**Figures 2 and 5** (`fig02_sensor_layouts.png`, `fig05_dyn04_forest.png`).

*What Figure 5 shows.* All 40 dynamic-optimal-versus-baseline contrasts at k = 1, 2, 3, 5 (rows: baseline and noise level). Blue points are wins that meet the registered criterion, grey are ties, orange are losses; the grey band is the 10 % minimum effect. The blue block against random and spread is the success; the grey/orange points in the high-population, static and source rows are the failures. Figure 2 shows where the layouts sit relative to the population: the high-population and designed layouts often coincide or neighbour each other, which is why they tie.

## 5. How many design scenarios are enough, and can a proxy be trusted? (Q5)

**Claim.** For the closed-form surrogate, the registered "90th-percentile regret <= 5 %" criterion is met from N = 24 (noise 0.10, k = 2: 4.8 %) and N = 48 (noise 0.02, k = 3), but **not met up to N = 192** in the other two cells (10.5 %; 23-47 %). With N <= 12 an ensemble that misses one wind gives a layout whose objective is about 30x the optimum [F-062; Report §6.5].

**What failed.** Under the real nonnegative estimator, ensemble layouts are +29 % / +34 % worse than the reference at N = 6 and still +14 % / +16 % at N = 192 for k = 2; the N = 6 vs N = 192 intervals include 0 in every cell, so the real estimator does **not show** that more design episodes help. The cell the surrogate certifies (0.10, k = 2, N >= 24) costs +28 % [8, 54] in the real estimator. The reference layout is itself noisy (split-half: +31 % [5, 71] worse than the best layout at 0.10/k = 3). Conclusion: an unconstrained-ridge surrogate cannot certify sufficiency for the constrained estimator. Disclosure: the independent reference was introduced after one summary had been seen and flipped one borderline cell. Q-013 for the Belauri objective is **[PENDING]**.

**Figure 6** (`fig06_dyn05_sufficiency.png`).

*What Figure 6 shows.* Top: the surrogate's 90th-percentile (blue) and mean (orange) relative regret against the number of design episodes N, one panel per (noise, k), with the registered 5 % line. Two panels cross the line and stay there; two never do (note the log scale and the non-monotone dip at N = 96 for noise 0.10, k = 3). Bottom: what the same ensembles cost under the real estimator: the excess error is positive with wide intervals and does not fall steadily, so the proxy and the real estimator disagree about where design is stable.

## 6. Can local changes be told apart from transboundary influx? (Q6)

**Claim.** Where a change is detected, attribution is right: with 4 sensors the pooled wrong-group rate at d = 16 is 0.00-0.02 for five scenarios; at d = 8 it is <= 0.08. The real 4-sensor limitation is **non-detection** in some winds: mean detection at d = 16 is 0.58-0.94 (S1: 0.58, clearly detected in only 4 of 6 winds), versus 0.79-1.00 with 9 sensors. A boundary-reference sensor gives no benefit (S1 pair: 0.04 vs 0.01 misattribution, detection 0.49 vs 0.58) [F-063; Report §6.6]. Some events are unobservable by construction: after a wind reversal the "influx ends" boundary basis has no inflow face (48 perfectly confounded cells).

**What failed / was contradicted.** P3 (>= 25 % misattribution at d = 2) is contradicted where evaluable (0.05-0.11) and mostly not evaluable (detection at the false-alarm level). The earlier headline "17-26 % misattribution at d = 16" was an averaging artefact and is withdrawn; P2's flip from "contradicted" to "holds" follows a scoring rule adopted after the first verdict (under the older metric S1/L4 is 0.31). No CIs on the confusion rates were stored.

**Figure 7** (`fig07_dyn06_detection_misattribution.png`).

*What Figure 7 shows.* Columns are layouts (4 sensors, 9 sensors, 4 sensors with an upwind-boundary reference sensor); top row is mean detection versus distinguishability d (how many noise standard deviations separate two look-alike scenarios), bottom row the pooled wrong-group rate where detection is clearly above the false-alarm level. Detection climbs slowly and incompletely with d at 4 sensors (S1 reaches only 0.58 at d = 16) but reaches about 0.9-1.0 with 9; the wrong-group rate falls to about 0-0.04 in all layouts at d = 16. Missing points at small d mean no wind was evaluable.

## 7. What does model error do? (Q7)

**Claim.** A 5 / 10 / 20 degree wind-direction error multiplies the 4-sensor error by 1.84 / 2.59 / 3.74 and the 9-sensor error by 2.72 / 4.51 / 7.33; the TV advantage at 4 sensors erodes from -20 % to -3 %; and beyond about 15-20 degrees the 9-sensor layout loses its absolute advantage over the 4-sensor one [F-056; Report §6.7]. Consistent with the earlier supported result that hidden wind error and an unmodelled source dominate discretisation mismatch [F-007].

**What we withdrew.** "More sensors tighten the fit to a wrong model" is not established: the unmodelled source in the extra-source test sits 354 m from a 9-sensor-layout sensor, which alone explains the x4.6 vs x1.4 cost. Absolute errors favour 9 sensors up to 10 degrees.

**Figure 8** (`fig08_dyn07_model_error.png`).

*What Figure 8 shows.* Left: how many times the error grows with wind rotation, for 4 and 9 sensors and both estimators (the 9-sensor curves rise faster). Middle: the same in absolute units; the 4- and 9-sensor curves cross between 10 and 20 degrees. Right: the M2-versus-M1 advantage under each perturbation: large when the model is right (-20 %), small for 9 sensors, and about -3 % at 20 degrees. Hyper-parameters were not re-tuned per condition, which is a conservative choice.

## 8. Is the mesh safe for sensor decisions? (Q8)

**Claim (negative).** EXP-027 is **void**. ## 10. What is established, what is not

* **Established (reviewed):** verified numerics (F-051); matched recovery, greedy vs spread from 3 sensors, wind error dominating discretisation mismatch, solver routing bit-identical (F-002, F-006, F-007, F-009).
* **Supported synthetic, preliminary:** causal history >> naive; dynamic-optimal >> random/spread; attribution right where detected, non-detection limits 4 sensors; wind error costs a factor 1.7-9; TV gain small and wind-concentrated.
* **Contradicted:** DYN-03 negative control; DYN-06 P3; DYN-05 H4 in 2 of 4 cells; dynamic-optimal beating the high-population heuristic at k >= 3.
* **Void / inconclusive:** EXP-027; mesh adequacy; Q-013.
* **Speculative:** carry-over as the mechanism; transfer to Belauri; generalisation of TV across winds; anything about real air quality.

## 11. Positioning (conservative)

Time-varying emission inversion, goal-oriented and population-weighted design, TV regularisation, boundary bias in regional inversions and sample-average design are all established (Sharrock & Kantas 2022; Attia et al. 2018; Wu-Chen-Ghattas 2023; Henriquez et al. 2015; Tibshirani et al. 2005; Lauvaux et al. 2012; Bhardwaj & Subramanian 2026 preprint). We claim **no novel method**. The defensible contributions are empirical: the structure-aware account of local-versus-boundary separability with non-detection as the real limit; the proxy-versus-real-estimator disagreement on design sufficiency; the verified cheap response framework; and the methodological record (falsification controls and independent review overturned eleven early findings). A systematic literature search remains to be done (Report §10, items marked "to verify"; Lin et al. 2026 could only be read in part).

## Likely questions (prepared answers)

* *"Is a 16-20 % gain worth it?"* On six winds, 4 sensors, matched model, concentrated in rotating winds; gone at 9 sensors and under about 20 degrees of wind error. We would not claim it without more winds and a second geometry.
* *"Why not trust the surrogate?"* It ignores nonnegativity and TV; Spearman 0.43 at k = 1; it certifies stability in cells where the real estimator shows +28 % error.
* *"Is the history result circular?"* Partly: the naive baseline ignores carry-over. The relevant contrasts are RETRO vs CAUSAL (future data) and M1 vs CAUSAL, which are -33 % to -59 %, and are not a causal-versus-noncausal fairness claim.
* *"What about real data?"* None used. This is a methods testbed.
