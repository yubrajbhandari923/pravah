# F-040 · DYN-07 model-error sensitivity (RUN-0065; the same 96 synthetic episodes, seeds 8200-8295, in 10 paired conditions; truth perturbed, inverse nominal, FROZEN DYN-02 v2 hyperparameters, 10% noise). All three registered predictions held. (P1) Error grows monotonically with wind rotation: at 4 sensors M1 x1.85, x2.56, x4.07 and M2 x1.97, x2.95, x4.95 of nominal at 5, 10, 20 degrees; at 9 sensors M1 x3.15, x5.36, x8.92. (P2) M2's advantage over the fair M1 at 4 sensors erodes with misspecification: -21% nominal, -15% / -9% / -3% at 5 / 10 / 20 degrees (paired CIs still exclude 0 but the effect is far below the 10% criterion beyond ~10 degrees), -7% in the combined condition; M2's mean degradation is slightly WORSE than M1's (x4.95 vs x4.07 at 20 degrees), so TV does not buy robustness. (P3) M2 beats the static M0 in a majority of episodes in every condition: 84-98% at 4 sensors, 59-99% at 9 sensors (lowest: unmodelled source at 30% of emission, 59%). The 9-sensor layout is MORE sensitive to model error than the 4-sensor layout (x8.9 vs x4.1 at 20 degrees; at 20 degrees M1 0.0150 vs M0 0.0204, i.e. only a 26% advantage over a static model), consistent with earlier findings that wind error dominates sensor count: extra sensors tighten the fit to a wrong model rather than the field. Speed error (0.8/1.2) cost x1.6-2.4, diffusion x2 cost x1.9-3.9, an unmodelled source of 10%/30% of the local emission cost x1.1-2.3 (4 sensors) and x2.3-5.5 (9 sensors). Caveats: one source geometry and population fixture; perturbations are global rotations/scalings of every wind segment; hyperparameters were deliberately not re-tuned (a model-error-aware tuning would likely help); no conditions are success criteria; preliminary, not independently reviewed; synthetic only.

## Statement

DYN-07 model-error sensitivity (RUN-0065; the same 96 synthetic episodes, seeds 8200-8295, in 10 paired conditions; truth perturbed, inverse nominal, FROZEN DYN-02 v2 hyperparameters, 10% noise). All three registered predictions held. (P1) Error grows monotonically with wind rotation: at 4 sensors M1 x1.85, x2.56, x4.07 and M2 x1.97, x2.95, x4.95 of nominal at 5, 10, 20 degrees; at 9 sensors M1 x3.15, x5.36, x8.92. (P2) M2's advantage over the fair M1 at 4 sensors erodes with misspecification: -21% nominal, -15% / -9% / -3% at 5 / 10 / 20 degrees (paired CIs still exclude 0 but the effect is far below the 10% criterion beyond ~10 degrees), -7% in the combined condition; M2's mean degradation is slightly WORSE than M1's (x4.95 vs x4.07 at 20 degrees), so TV does not buy robustness. (P3) M2 beats the static M0 in a majority of episodes in every condition: 84-98% at 4 sensors, 59-99% at 9 sensors (lowest: unmodelled source at 30% of emission, 59%). The 9-sensor layout is MORE sensitive to model error than the 4-sensor layout (x8.9 vs x4.1 at 20 degrees; at 20 degrees M1 0.0150 vs M0 0.0204, i.e. only a 26% advantage over a static model), consistent with earlier findings that wind error dominates sensor count: extra sensors tighten the fit to a wrong model rather than the field. Speed error (0.8/1.2) cost x1.6-2.4, diffusion x2 cost x1.9-3.9, an unmodelled source of 10%/30% of the local emission cost x1.1-2.3 (4 sensors) and x2.3-5.5 (9 sensors). Caveats: one source geometry and population fixture; perturbations are global rotations/scalings of every wind segment; hyperparameters were deliberately not re-tuned (a model-error-aware tuning would likely help); no conditions are success criteria; preliminary, not independently reviewed; synthetic only.
